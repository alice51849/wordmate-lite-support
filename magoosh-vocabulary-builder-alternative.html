Five things to check in any replacement

  • Recent updates. Look at the version history date on the App Store page.
  • Your goal. A test-prep word list and a daily vocabulary habit are different tools; pick the one that matches why you study.
  • Daily load. A small daily goal you keep beats a big review queue you abandon.
  • Offline use. Commutes and flights are where short study sessions happen.
  • Privacy. Check whether you need an account and what data the app collects.

Who Wordmate Lite is not for

Wordmate Lite is not a dedicated GRE word list. It builds a daily vocabulary habit across 14 life paths, including exams, business, travel and daily life, at beginner, intermediate and advanced level. If you need one specific test's official word list, use that test's own materials alongside it.

Switching in one minute

  1. Install Wordmate Lite and pick English (or any of the 44 languages).
  2. Choose a path and level. Exams or business at intermediate or advanced level is a natural start for former test-prep users.
  3. Walk today's five-word trail. The closing screen shows the exact five words you learned.
  4. Save the words worth keeping and set a daily goal to keep your streak going.

Privacy and purchase

Everything runs offline. No account, no sign in, no personal data collected, no third-party advertising, no tracking. You can start without paying: one learning language and one path at beginner level stay open for good. A single one-time in-app purchase unlocks all 44 languages, all 14 paths, every level, unlimited learning and an unlimited saved word list. There is no subscription.
